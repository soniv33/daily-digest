crossing $100 a barrel for the first time since May, after Houthi militants in Yemen claimed to have struck two Saudi tankers in the Red Sea, an attack that threatened to worsen an oil shock already among the largest in history.
 
Brent crude settled above $100 per barrel for the first time in two months, rising about 7% to $100.69, as widening conflict raised fears about inflation and market volatility.
 The US carried out a thirteenth consecutive night of strikes on Iran, and 
Tehran activated its air defenses, with explosions reported over the islands of Qeshm, Larak and Hengam, and the Iranian cities of Ahvaz and Bandar Abbas.
 This is no longer a distant war for Britain, either. 
The UK said its armed forces are ready to protect the country after Iran warned that any base used to launch US bomber strikes, including RAF Fairford, would be considered a legitimate target.
 Against that backdrop, Netanyahu is due in Washington next week for his first meeting with Trump since the war began.

That escalation is now hitting money markets directly. 
The S&P 500 is down about 2% since consecutive US strikes began on July 12, and major indexes tumbled Thursday as investors priced in a prolonged conflict.
 
West Texas Intermediate jumped 6% to $92 a barrel, up more than 28% from lows below $70 earlier this month.
 Strategists at Wells Fargo now say 
traders need to reconsider the economic fears they had back in March, worried about both higher inflation and a hit to consumer spending, with the reignited conflict reason enough to brace for a larger equity drawdown.
 Britain isn't insulated: the Bank of England's rate decision lands July 30, and 
Governor Andrew Bailey has already warned that recent energy price increases are likely to keep feeding through into inflation, noting that four months of higher energy costs mean pressure is already in the pipeline.


Away from the war, OpenAI took a step toward public markets. 
It appointed BNY's Robin Vince and Nubank's David Vélez to its boards, adding financial-services heavyweights as it moves toward an IPO, following its confidential prospectus filing with the SEC in June.
 
The company carries a valuation of $852 billion, with Goldman Sachs and Morgan Stanley leading the filing process ahead of a potential fall listing.
 It matters because the AI boom is about to face public shareholders and quarterly scrutiny for the first time, just as the broader market turns nervous.
