The Iran war has flared back up, and markets spent Wednesday absorbing the shock.


US Central Command says it completed a heavy wave of strikes on dozens of Revolutionary Guard targets in Iran, a day after Iran's military targeted American troops in the region with ballistic missiles, hitting command centers, missile and drone facilities.
 Separately, 
US-Saudi strikes on Iraq killed at least 20 members of the Iran-backed Iraqi Popular Mobilization Forces.
 Iran, meanwhile, is suspected of hitting back elsewhere: 
two unnamed Iranian sources told the New York Times that Tehran was behind a drone attack on a US-owned gas tanker in Egypt, the first targeting of that country in the current war.
 The fighting began in February when the US and Israel launched strikes that killed Iran's supreme leader, and a June ceasefire has now unraveled.

Markets moved fast. 
Brent crude leaped five and a half percent to more than eighty six dollars a barrel after fighting resumed.
 
The Dow closed over 1,150 points lower, its worst decline since April 2025, while the S&P 500 slid one and a half percent and the Nasdaq fell nearly two percent, more than ten percent off its record high.


That selloff coincided with the Federal Reserve's decision. 
The Fed voted nine-to-three to hold its key rate at three and a half to three and three quarters percent, with three regional presidents dissenting because inflation has stayed above target for more than five years.
 
Officials again cited elevated uncertainty tied to the Middle East conflict.
 
Treasury yields spiked in response, with the 30-year hitting its highest level since 2007.


The Bank of England faces the identical squeeze today. 
UK inflation eased to a 15-month low of two point six percent in June,
 yet policymakers are still expected to hold rates, weighing that easing price pressure against the same Middle East-driven energy shock.

In AI, Microsoft and Meta delivered a lesson in diverging fortunes. 
Microsoft still managed to increase profits while Meta posted a sharp earnings decline; Meta shares slumped six percent in after-hours trading, while Microsoft rose two percent.
 
Meta raised its 2026 capital spending forecast to as much as 145 billion dollars,
 underscoring investor unease about how much Big Tech is spending to chase AI, with no clear payoff timeline yet.
