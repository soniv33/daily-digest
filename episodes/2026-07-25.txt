The most consequential story right now is the war between the United States and Iran, now in its fifth month, and its direct hit on markets and the wider region.

President Trump said today the US remains, in his words, 
prepared to intensify military operations while expressing optimism that Iran was becoming more serious about reaching an agreement, adding "we're locked and loaded and ready to go, but we're talking to them."
 He framed it as a choice between two paths: 
a "military exit where we just keep going" or "a smarter strategy" through a deal, though he said "I just don't think they're ready yet."
 Iran's foreign minister Abbas Araghchi responded that 
Tehran does not fear threats or succumb to pressure.


The fighting has widened well beyond Iran itself. 
Saudi air defenses intercepted two ballistic missiles launched from Yemen targeting oil refineries in Yanbu, after the Houthis vowed retaliation against the kingdom.
 Separately, 
Bahrain and Kuwait have secretly carried out airstrikes on Iranian drone and missile sites, marking their first direct retaliation against Tehran.


Markets are no longer shrugging this off. 
Brent crude traded near one hundred dollars a barrel on Friday, on track to gain nearly fourteen percent for the week, after the US launched a thirteenth straight day of strikes on Iran with both sides ruling out near-term talks.
 It did ease back Friday, with 
Brent falling roughly four percent to settle near ninety-seven dollars, its biggest one-day drop since late June.
 Wall Street has been rattled: 
the S&P 500 is now down about two percent since the consecutive evening strikes by the US began on July 12.


That energy volatility is now feeding directly into inflation. 
UK inflation was 2.6% in June, above the Bank of England's target, though before the Middle East conflict the Bank had expected it to settle near 2% for the rest of the year.
 
The European Central Bank left rates unchanged at 2.25% this week, following a June increase driven by inflation pressure from the conflict.
 The Bank of England delivers its next rate decision on July 30.
